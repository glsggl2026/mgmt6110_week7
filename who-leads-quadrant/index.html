My agent card | Appointment Reply Drafter | finished 17 Oct 2026, 12:00 PM SGT

  1. The job: When someone emails me to ask for an appointment, it drafts a short, polite reply that greets them by first name and responds to what they asked. If they want to meet in the future, it invites them to book a time on my shared Google schedule; if the request isn’t realistic, it replies politely. It works for me, a postgrad student who juggles postgrad timelines, personal life and project meetings, so senders can book directly and I spend less time on rescheduling.
  2. The trigger: A new email in my Gmail Inbox with “appointment” in the subject (Gmail – New Email Matching Search: in:inbox subject:appointment), checked about every 15 minutes.
  3. The tools: Gmail – New Email Matching Search (reads the request); AI by Zapier (writes the reply wording, no tools attached); Gmail – Create Draft Reply (saves the draft in the same conversation, To: the sender, with my booking link and sign-off as fixed text).
  4. It must never: Send an email without my review. The setting that stops it: the Zap’s only Gmail action is Create Draft Reply. It has no Send Email or Reply action, so it cannot send.
  5. Where a person checks: I (the account owner) open each draft in Gmail before sending it, and check that the greeting uses the right name, the reply invents no dates or times and does not accept a date that has already passed, the booking link opens, and the draft sits in the right conversation. I also check whether the reply needs more, based on what their email says, and edit it before I send it or delete it.

Runs, tricky case and fix

The Zap ran on 16 Oct (18:11, 18:40), 17 Oct (15:03) and 18 Oct (11:13, 11:49, 12:14). I checked each run on Zapier’s Zap runs page, comparing what the trigger caught (sender, subject, Thread Id) with what the draft step received, then opened the draft in Gmail.

Q1. Where does my agent sit on the map?

(a) Quadrant

A person leads, AI inside (top left).

(b) The two questions

Right answer? Partly, and the parts that decide whether a reply is acceptable have right answers: the right person is greeted, my real booking link is included, the draft sits in the sender’s conversation, and it promises no time I cannot keep. The exact wording has no single right answer, but wording is not what makes this reply right or wrong, so I place it in the right-answer column. In the 18 Oct 12:14 run, the draft greeted the sender by name, carried the correct link, and sat in the sender’s own conversation.

Together? Yes. The AI drafts the wording, but only I can judge whether the reply fits the request and my real commitments, and the reply goes out in my name. My runs show why: in the tricky case (18 Oct 12:14), asked to meet at the 2025 Singapore F1, the AI did not promise the date but did not point out that it had passed; and on 18 Oct 11:13 a draft landed in the wrong conversation. I caught both at the draft.

(c) The person in the loop

I stand at the gate in Gmail Drafts, before anything is sent. For each draft I check the name, that no date or time is invented or already past, that the link opens, and that the draft is in the right conversation, and I add anything the email asks for that the reply misses. On its own, the agent may only read emails with “appointment” in the subject, write the reply wording, and save a draft. It has no send action, so it cannot send.

It would move to AI alone, with checks (bottom left) if the AI could read my calendar, my calendar were always up to date, and the AI could reply in a human tone, spot errors in the request such as a past date, and show empathy. Then the reply would be right often enough for me to spot-check instead of reviewing every draft.

It would move to A person decides, AI alongside (bottom right) if my schedule had no fixed weekly free slots and I had no booking link to share. Every request would then have to be weighed against my current commitments, which is work with no single right answer that I would decide myself, with the AI only helping with wording.

Reflection

The agent fitted a repetitive part of my week: each appointment request gets a polite, personalised draft with my booking link in its own conversation, so I only review and send. It failed where the cheapest AI has no tools or judgement: it cannot see my calendar, it did not notice that F1 2025 had passed, and until I mapped the Thread ID a draft landed in the wrong conversation. I would give the AI the date each email was received and, on a higher tier, access to my calendar, so it can flag impossible dates and offer real free slots.
